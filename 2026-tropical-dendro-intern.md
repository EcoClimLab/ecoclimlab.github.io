**Research Project** 

Understanding the relationship between climate and tree woody growth allows us to project the future of forest carbon (C) uptake under climate change.
In the tropics, the relationship between climate and tree growth remains a research frontier because only a limited number of tree species form anatomically distinct annual rings that provide the type of long-term growth record needed to characterize climate sensitivity.
ForestGEO has been using dendrometer bands to measure growth of thousands of tropical trees for more than 15 years, providing a record suitable for characterizing the climate sensitivity of growth across diverse species and climates.
In this project, we will use long-term records of seasonal and annual tree growth from tropical ForestGEO sites to understand diverse tropical tree responses to interannual climatic variation. 
Questions that the intern may help address include: How does growth vary seasonally across species and climates?; How do responses to climatic variables vary across sites and species?; Which trees are likely to be favored or disadvantaged as the climate warms, and what are their characteristics (in terms of species traits, ranges, and evolutionary history and individual size and microenvironment within the forest)? 
To address these questions, the intern will work with mentors and collaborators to organize and analyze existing tropical tree dendrometer data and to develop results into a scientific publication.

**Research Activities and Learning Opportunities**

The intern will gain experience analyzing tree growth dynamics and will build experience in the following areas: 

- Analyze long-term dendrometer datasets to characterize seasonal and annual tree growth.
- Investigate relationships between tree growth and climate variables. 
- Compare growth responses across species, sites, and environmental conditions.
- Develop and apply statistical approaches to address ecological research questions.
- Improve data analysis skills in R and scientific workflow management in GitHub.
- Learn about tree diversity and carbon sequestration of tropical forests.
- Contribute to a scientific publication as co-author or lead author depending on the quality and extent of the intern's contributions.
- Collaborate internationally with ForestGEO researchers.
- Gain exposure to international science and conservation programs through ForestGEO and NZCBI.
- Receive mentorship in developing a scientific career, including preparation for and application to graduate school. Interns in this lab have an outstanding track record of graduate school acceptance.
