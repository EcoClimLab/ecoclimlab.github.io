# Smithsonian Postdoctoral Fellowship in Temperate Forest Biomass Dynamics – ForestGEO and GEO-TREES

ForestGEO and GEO-TREES seek an ambitious postdoctoral researcher to develop an independent research program on temperate forest biomass dynamics using one of the world's largest networks of long-term forest research plots, new terrestrial and airborne laser-scanning observations, and satellite biomass products.
The position is hosted by Smithsonian’s National Zoo and Conservation Biology Institute (NZCBI), the Smithsonian Environmental Research Center (SERC), and the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute (STRI).

The Forest Global Earth Observatory ([ForestGEO](forestgeo.si.edu)) is a global network of long-term forest research plots and scientists dedicated to the study of forest function and diversity.
ForestGEO conducts long-term, large-scale research on forests around the world to increase scientific understanding of forest ecosystems, guide sustainable forest management and natural-resource policies, monitor the impacts of environmental change, and build capacity in forest science.
ForestGEO is one of several institutions involved in [GEO-TREES](https://www.geo-trees.org/), a global partnership of forest researchers working to provide critical ground observations for calibration of satellite-derived biomass mapping.
Initially focused on the tropics, GEO-TREES is now seeking to expand to provide coverage of temperate forests. 

Temperate forests account for almost half of the net global forest carbon sink and are facing unprecedented pressures from climate and other environmental changes.
At the same time, carbon markets require accurate quantification of forest biomass and its stability in the face of ongoing environmental changes.
Long-term ForestGEO data, coupled with more recent and future acquisitions of terrestrial and airborne laser scanning data following GEO-TREES protocols, will be used to explore the drivers and dynamics of temperate forest biomass dynamics and to scale between ground-based observations and satellite biomass estimates.
The overall goal is to significantly improve the quantification of temperate forest biomass and its future vulnerability. 

The postdoctoral fellow will have the opportunity to build an independent research program aligned with the goals of ForestGEO and GEO-TREES.
The position offers the rare opportunity to connect global forest census data with state-of-the-art lidar data and emerging satellite biomass products.
The fellow will work across spatial and temporal scales—from individual trees and permanent plots to landscapes and satellite products—and will have substantial freedom to develop questions and approaches that establish an independent scientific niche.
Research topics may include, but are not limited to, scaling plot-level observations to landscape or global scales, global change impacts on biomass trajectories, or linking forest structure to biomass dynamics.
Candidates are encouraged to propose questions that leverage the unique strengths of the ForestGEO and GEO-TREES data and research infrastructure.
Under the mentorship of Smithsonian ForestGEO and GEO-TREES researchers, they will define and lead their own analyses, initiate collaborations, lead publications, contribute to and potentially lead grant proposals, and (co-)supervise interns. 

We seek a creative and analytically strong scientist with a PhD in forest ecology, environmental science, remote sensing, Earth system science, or a related field.
They should be enthusiastic about collaborating across cultures and disciplines and have a strong research and publication record.
Candidates with background in any combination of ground-based forest census, terrestrial and airborne laser scanning, and satellite biomass estimation are encouraged to apply.
We particularly welcome applicants who bring depth in one area and enthusiasm for developing expertise across the others. 

The position will be based in the ForestGEO Ecosystems & Climate Lab at the Smithsonian’s National Zoo & Conservation Biology Institute in Front Royal, VA, within easy reach of Washington, DC and the broader Mid-Atlantic scientific community.
The Smithsonian Conservation Biology Institute and the Smithsonian Environmental Research Center (Edgewater, MD) are home to the first two temperate GEO-TREES sites and will be foundational sites for the postdoc’s research.
The postdoc will interact closely with ForestGEO and GEO-TREES collaborators at other Smithsonian units and in international partner institutions. They will have opportunities to travel for scientific conferences and research collaborations across temperate and tropical forest countries involved in the ForestGEO and GEO-TREES programs.

The fellow will be awarded a stipend of $70,000/yr plus a $7,000/yr health coverage allowance and $5,000/yr research and travel allowance, with additional funds available to support the position at PI discretion.
The position is funded for a minimum of two years, with potential for extension based on availability of funds. The starting date is flexible; earlier start dates are preferred.

Closing date for applications is **26 October 2026**.
